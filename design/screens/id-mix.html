How this works
Strongest source winsMixing with a keyed hash means the seed is at least as unpredictable as the best single source. A weak or broken source can't make it worse; it can only be dropped.
Never from you aloneBrocast refuses to make a key from your scribble alone (the “brain wallet” mistake), and stops if fewer than two machine sources work.
Everything comes from the seed by hashingEach key is a hash of the seed with its own label, so none of them can be worked back to the seed or to each other.
Clamping, and why your seed isn't touchedEd25519 and X25519 clear and set a few bits of a hash of the seed, as their standards require, to keep keys away from weak points on the curve. That's where their ~128-bit strength comes from, and it costs nothing. Brocast never clamps the seed itself, and never uses the library calls that would clamp a key twice.
StoredThe seed is encrypted with a key held in the phone's security chip (StrongBox) and kept in Brocast's private files. Sensor readings were wiped after mixing; location-bearing ones in the same instant.
